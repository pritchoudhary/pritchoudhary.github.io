What pulls it back?

A spring pulls toward equilibrium with force −kx. A damper opposes velocity with force −cv. Together they produce acceleration (−kx − cv) / m.

Why does it feel different?

The damping ratio ζ = c / 2√(mk) separates oscillation, critical damping and a slower non-oscillating response. The browser integrator uses a fixed 1/240 second step; numerical approximation still introduces error.

What is validated?

This is a JavaScript teaching model with corresponding C# source and saved Unity scenes. It is not an embedded Unity WebGL build. Clean final exported-player validation remains pending.

At 1× speed, both briefs have achievable damper settings. At other speeds, meeting every target is not guaranteed. Targets are invented learning constraints, not vehicle safety limits. “Settled” means body position stays within ±2 mm and velocity within ±5 mm/s for the rest of this 8-second simulation. Relative travel is body position minus road position.

04 / USE THIS IDEA

Game suspension prototyping

Use spring compression and relative velocity to create a tunable vertical force. This lab helps explain why changing a damper affects both bounce and impact transmission.

In a Unity vehicle, measure each wheel’s compression and relative velocity in the physics step. Apply its spring and damper force at the suspension point; then test tire contact, body rotation, and travel limits separately.

TRANSFER THE LEARNING

A camera has a different brief

A follow camera also trades fast tracking against overshoot. But its target is a character, not a road; its units and comfort constraints differ. Reuse the reasoning, not these coefficients.

Try this thought experiment: if a target changes direction mid-motion, should the camera preserve velocity or snap? A spring preserves continuity; a precise aiming system may need an immediate response.

WHERE THIS MODEL FAILS

A wheel can leave the ground

This model cannot. It has one moving mass and a prescribed wheel position. It omits tire deformation, wheel mass, contact loss, bump stops, pitch, and roll.

Do not use its results to size real suspension components or predict grip. The planned full suspension project needs additional degrees of freedom and contact validation.

Show the model, assumptions, and a next experiment

m z″ = −k(z − r) − c(z′ − r′)

z is body displacement and r is road height, measured from gravity-loaded equilibrium. The road is an 80 mm sin² bump beginning at t = 1 second, lasting 0.50 seconds at the selected 1.00× crossing speed. The bump geometry is fixed; doubling speed halves the crossing duration. Spring force depends on relative displacement; damping force depends on relative velocity. The browser uses fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 240 steps per second.

ζ = c / (2√mk) describes the unforced response. Critical damping does not guarantee the smallest acceleration under a moving-road input. Try the speed experiment: hold the damper fixed and compare 0.5×, 1× and 2×. Predict how a shorter, faster road input changes the force from the velocity term, then inspect the measured peak and trace.
